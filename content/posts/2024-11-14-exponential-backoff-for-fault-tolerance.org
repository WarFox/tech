Exponential backoff is a technique useful for networked applications to cope
with temporary service unavailability.

* Kafka

* Microservice

* Front end applications

* Airflow

We are missing =max_retry_delay= in that configuration,
https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/1.10.9/_api/airflow/operators/index.html

=max_retry_delay= sets an upper limit on the delay between retries. It is
essential to set =max_retry_delay= when =retry_exponential_backoff= set to =True=
otherwise the delays between retries can grow to several hours.

For example with the following configuration

#+begin_src yaml
retries: 6
retry_delay: 1 minute (initial delay)
retry_exponential_backoff: True
max_retry_delay: timedelta(minutes=10)
#+end_src

#+begin_src
  1st retry: 1 minute, total = 1 minute
  2nd retry: 2 minutes (1 minute * 2), total = 3 minutes
  3rd retry: 4 minutes (2 minutes * 2), total = 7 minutes
  4th retry: 8 minutes (4 minutes * 2), total = 15 minutes
  5th retry: 10 minutes (capped by max_retry_delay, otherwise it would be 8*2=16 minutes ), total = 25 / 31 minutes
  6th retry: 10 minutes (capped by max_retry_delay, otherwise it would be 16*2=32 minutes ), total = 35 / 63 minutes
#+end_src

Without =exponential_backoff=, retries will be done at constant interval of =retry_delay= like the following

#+begin_src
  1st retry: 1 minute, total = 1 minute
  2nd retry: 1 minute, total = 2 minutes
  3rd retry: 1 minute, total = 3 minutes
  4th retry: 1 minute, total = 4 minutes
  5th retry: 1 minute, total = 5 minutes
  6th retry: 1 minutes, total = 6 minutes
#+end_src

Carefully configured exponential backoff prevents network congestion, reduce
load on strained services, handle transient faults that is taking longer that
expected etc.
